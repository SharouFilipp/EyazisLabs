Romantic Poetry

Romanticism is a literary movement that appeared at the end of the eighteenth century. The romantic poets rejected the strict rules of classicism. They believed that emotion is more important than reason.

Nature occupies a central place in romantic poetry. The poets describe mountains, forests and the sea. For them, nature reflects the feelings of the soul. The landscape often expresses sadness or hope.

The romantic hero is a lonely individual. He feels that society does not understand him. He dreams of an ideal world and suffers because reality disappoints him.

In England, William Wordsworth and Lord Byron were important representatives of this movement. In France, Alphonse de Lamartine and Victor Hugo wrote famous poems. Their works express love, melancholy and the passage of time.

The romantic movement changed the language of poetry. The poets used simple words and personal images. This freedom influenced many writers of the following centuries.
