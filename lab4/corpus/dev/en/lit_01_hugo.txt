Les Miserables by Victor Hugo

Les Miserables is a novel by Victor Hugo. The author published the book in 1862. The novel tells the story of Jean Valjean, a former prisoner who tries to begin a new life.

Valjean stole a loaf of bread because his family was hungry. He spent nineteen years in prison. After his release, a bishop shows him kindness and gives him a second chance. This meeting changes the life of the hero.

The main theme of the novel is justice. Hugo shows that poverty and ignorance create crime. The inspector Javert represents the law, but he does not understand mercy. In contrast, Valjean represents love and compassion.

The novel also describes the history of France and the life of the poor people of Paris. The character of Fantine shows the suffering of women in society. Her daughter Cosette finds happiness thanks to Valjean.

Hugo writes with great emotion. His long descriptions sometimes slow down the action, but they give the reader a deep picture of the epoch. Today the novel remains one of the most famous works of French literature.
