1984 by George Orwell

1984 is a dystopian novel by George Orwell. The writer published the book in 1949. The story takes place in a totalitarian state where the Party controls every aspect of life.

The main character, Winston Smith, works at the Ministry of Truth. His job is to change old newspapers. The Party constantly rewrites history, so the past does not exist anymore. Big Brother watches the citizens through screens in every room.

Winston secretly hates the Party. He keeps a diary and falls in love with Julia. Their relationship is an act of rebellion. However, the police arrest them, and the torturers break the will of Winston.

The novel shows how a totalitarian regime destroys freedom of thought. The language of the Party reduces the number of words, so people cannot express dangerous ideas. This idea is one of the most original aspects of the book.

The author wrote a warning to future generations. Today many readers compare the world of the novel with modern society. The book remains relevant because it raises questions about truth and power.
