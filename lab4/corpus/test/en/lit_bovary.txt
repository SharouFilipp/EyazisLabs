Madame Bovary by Gustave Flaubert

Madame Bovary is a novel by Gustave Flaubert. The book appeared in 1857 and caused a scandal. The author was accused of offending public morality.

Emma, the heroine, grew up on a farm and read many romantic novels. She marries Charles Bovary, a simple country doctor. Soon she feels that her life is empty and boring. She dreams of luxury, passion and adventure.

Emma looks for happiness in love affairs. She also buys expensive objects and accumulates debts. Her lovers abandon her, and the creditors threaten her family. Desperate, she poisons herself.

Flaubert criticizes the illusions of romantic literature. He shows the conflict between dreams and the banal reality of provincial life. Charles is a good man, but he does not understand the desires of his wife.

The style of the novel is very precise. Flaubert worked for years on each sentence. For this reason, Madame Bovary is often considered the first modern realist novel.
