Crime and Punishment by Fyodor Dostoevsky

Crime and Punishment is a novel by Fyodor Dostoevsky. The writer published it in 1866. The story takes place in Saint Petersburg.

The hero, Raskolnikov, is a poor former student. He believes that extraordinary people have the right to break the moral law. To prove this theory, he kills an old woman who lends money.

After the murder, Raskolnikov suffers from fear and guilt. He becomes ill and avoids his friends. The investigator Porfiry suspects him, but he has no evidence.

Raskolnikov meets Sonia, a young woman who sacrifices herself for her family. Her faith and her kindness touch him. Finally, he confesses his crime and goes to prison in Siberia. Sonia follows him there.

The novel explores the psychology of a criminal. Dostoevsky shows that a man cannot escape his conscience. The book also raises questions about suffering, redemption and faith.
